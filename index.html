Water damage restoration in Athens, Ohio: what it means

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping the source, removing standing water, drying wet structure and contents to a measured goal, and repairing what could not be saved. In Athens, OH that work happens in older one-story homes near the Hocking River, in rented houses around Ohio University, and in downtown buildings along East State Street and North Court Street.

This page covers the whole job from the first call to the final repair. The nineteen service pages linked below go deeper on a single source, such as a burst pipe, or a single material, such as hardwood.

When water is on the floor, the first hour matters

Water keeps spreading after the leak stops. It wicks up drywall, runs under flooring and soaks into the pad beneath carpet. Every hour it sits, more material has to be removed instead of dried.

The 24/7 emergency line is answered at night, on weekends and on holidays. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, and the dispatcher tells you plainly what to expect on the call.

If water is still spreading, do this while you wait

These steps are safe for most households. Skip any step that puts you near standing water and electricity.

1

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve for the fixture, or the main water valve if you cannot find it. If the water is coming from the roof or outside, move belongings away from it.

2

Make the area safe

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call the electric utility or 911 before calling us.

3

Protect what you can

Lift valuables, shoes and electronics off the floor. Pull rugs and cushions away from the wet area. Keep children and pets out of any contaminated water.

4

Photograph and call

Take photos of the water, the source and the damage. Then call the 24/7 line and describe what you see, including where it started.

Describe the water and the room. The dispatcher will tell you what happens next.

The first 72 hours, step by step

A restoration job follows a predictable order. Knowing it helps you judge whether the plan you are given makes sense.

Hours 0 to 4: stop and assess

The source is stopped, power and safety are checked, and moisture meters and thermal imaging show how far the water traveled. The water category is identified, because contaminated water changes what can be kept.

Hours 4 to 24: remove water

Extraction units and wet vacuums pull standing water. Soaked pad, loose insulation and wet baseboards are removed when drying them in place will not work.

Days 1 to 3: dry the structure

Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers are set according to the readings. The crew returns to check moisture daily and moves or removes equipment as the readings fall.

Day 3 onward: confirm and repair

Readings are compared with the drying goal for the material. Only then does repair begin, from replacing drywall sections to refinishing a floor.

How restoration is priced and how insurance fits in

Nobody can name an honest price for water damage before seeing it. Two flooded rooms can need very different work. The scope and price are explained before work starts, and the factors below are what move the number.

The same leak can be a one-day dry-out or a multi-week rebuild.

Why Athens water damage is shaped by the Hocking River

Athens sits in the Hocking River valley, and the city and Ohio University grew up on a floodplain. According to Ohio University, the river overflowed so often before 1970 that campus flooding was treated as an annual event, and the 1964 and 1968 floods are still remembered by alumni. The U.S. Army Corps of Engineers rerouted and widened the channel starting in 1969, moving about 3.9 million cubic yards of earth to do it.

The rerouting reduced river flooding in town, but it did not repeal the valley. Heavy rain still runs off the surrounding hills toward low ground, so basements, crawl spaces and ground-floor units near the river and its creeks are where groundwater and runoff show up first. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the City of Athens about floodplain rules before you rebuild in a mapped area.

Flood insurance matters here for the same reason. The Ohio Department of Insurance notes that flood insurance is typically not included in most homeowners, renters and business policies and that a 30-day waiting period usually applies before a new flood policy starts. If you are reading this before a storm, that waiting period is worth knowing about.

Homes and buildings we see in Athens

Athens has a mix that changes how water damage plays out. Many blocks near downtown and along the East State Street corridor are older houses, and older houses tend to have shallow crawl spaces, cellars and aging supply lines. Roughly three in four occupied homes in the city are rentals, according to Census figures, so a leak often involves a tenant, a landlord and an insurer at once.

Larger buildings carry their own issues. Older buildings in the downtown historic district on North Court Street and apartment buildings near campus have long runs of pipe and large roofs, so a single failure can reach several units. The commercial water damage page explains how crews work in occupied buildings.

Frequently asked questions

How fast can someone get to my home in Athens?

Dispatch is answered 24 hours a day, and same-day response is offered when crews are available. No company can honestly promise an arrival time before it knows where the crews are and what the roads look like, so the dispatcher gives you a real answer on the call instead of a number printed on a web page.

While you wait, stop the source if you can reach it safely, and photograph the water before anything is moved.

How much notice do you need, and can someone just come and look at it?

No advance notice is needed. Call when you see the problem. A visit starts with moisture readings and a look at where the water came from, then the scope of work and the price are explained before anything is torn out or set up.

If you only want to understand what you are dealing with, say so on the call. Many calls begin as a question about a stain, a soft floor or a smell.

Does homeowners insurance pay for water damage in Athens?

It depends on the cause and on your policy. Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst supply line, is often covered. Gradual leaks and neglected maintenance often are not. Flood damage from rising water is usually excluded and needs a separate flood policy, which the Ohio Department of Insurance notes is typically not part of a standard homeowners, renters or business policy.

Read your policy or ask your insurer before you decide what to claim. Nobody outside your insurance company can promise that a claim will be approved.

What should I photograph for my insurance company?

Take wide shots of each affected room, then close-ups of the source, the wet materials and any damaged belongings, before anything is moved or thrown away. Keep a list of items with rough ages and replacement costs.

The crew also records moisture readings and equipment placement during the job. Those logs and photos give your adjuster a dated record of what was wet and how it was dried.

Do you rent drying equipment without doing the job?

Equipment rental is not offered here. Air movers and dehumidifiers only work when they are placed against moisture readings and checked every day, which is why drying is done as a service with monitoring rather than as a drop-off.

Is restoring water damage the same as waterproofing a basement?

No. Restoration removes water and dries and repairs what got wet. Waterproofing changes the grading, drainage or foundation so that water stops entering. If a basement takes on water every time it rains, the restoration crew can dry it, but a drainage or foundation contractor needs to fix the cause.

Which towns do you serve besides Athens?

The service area includes The Plains, Chauncey, Nelsonville, Millfield and Amesville. Calls from other places in Athens County and nearby are handled case by case, so call and ask.

Do I need to be home when the crew arrives?

Someone with authority to approve the work should be there at the start so the scope and price can be agreed. After that, access can be arranged. Tenants should call their landlord before authorizing work in a rental unit.
