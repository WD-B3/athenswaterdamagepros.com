Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Athens, OH

Ice often hides the damage until the thaw. When the pipe starts leaking, shut off the water and call the 24/7 line.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

A frozen pipe becomes an emergency when it thaws. Ice can split a pipe weeks before anyone notices, and the water only pours out once the ice melts. If you see water after a cold snap, shut off the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call the 24/7 line.

This page covers damage that starts with ice blocking a pipe. Water from a pipe that burst under pressure without freezing is on the burst pipe page. In practice the cleanup is similar, but the cause, the locations and the prevention differ.

When a thaw turns into an emergency

Ice does not usually break a pipe at the point where it forms. Water trapped between the ice block and a closed faucet builds pressure and splits the pipe wall somewhere along that stretch. The split sits closed while the pipe is frozen, then opens as the temperature rises.

Winter nights in the Athens area regularly drop below freezing, and a mild afternoon can turn a quiet split into a running leak. If you hear water behind a wall or see a stain spreading on a ceiling after a cold night, call right away. Same-day response is offered when crews are available, including nights and weekends.

If the pipe is still frozen

Do not use a torch or open flame to thaw a pipe. A hair dryer on low heat or warm towels can work, but if you suspect a split, leave the pipe frozen, shut off the main valve and call a plumber and the emergency line. Thawing a split pipe releases the water.

Where frozen pipes happen in Athens homes

Pipes fail in places the heat does not reach. Exterior walls, crawl spaces, attached garages, unheated porches and runs through outbuildings are the usual suspects. Pipes near a window, or in a bathroom added to the back of an older house, also freeze when a cold front hits.

Older houses near downtown and along East State Street often have additions that put plumbing in thinner, less insulated walls. Rentals left half-heated over a break are another common case, because nobody is watching the thermostat.

If your problem is a leak in the crawl space, the crawl space water page explains how crews handle the void under the floor.

How restoration works after a freeze split

The first task is stopping the water and confirming the pipe is isolated. Then a moisture meter and thermal camera find the wet zones. Freeze splits tend to be hidden, so the wet area is often larger than the visible stain.

Crews extract standing water, open the wall or ceiling where the line runs when readings show water inside, and set air movers and dehumidifiers to dry what stays. Insulation is usually replaced because it holds water, while framing is dried and checked before it is covered again.

Once readings reach the goal, drywall, paint and trim are replaced. A plumber should repair or replace the split section, and insulating the line afterward is the way to stop a repeat.

Why the damage often looks small at first

A freeze split can leak slowly, and the water goes down through the wall instead of onto the floor. By the time a stain shows on a ceiling or a baseboard swells, the cavity behind it has been wet for hours or days.

The sooner you call after a cold snap shows signs of water, the less drywall and insulation need to be removed. A faucet that runs weakly or not at all after a freeze is also a hint that a pipe is blocked or split. Shut off the main valve when in doubt.

Once a frozen pipe thaws and leaks, the response follows ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage practice. The Ohio Department of Insurance also publishes consumer guidance on winter claims, which is worth reading before you file.

Preventing a second freeze

  • Insulate exposed pipes in crawl spaces, garages and exterior walls.
  • Keep the heat on in vacant units and rentals, even at a low setting.
  • Let faucets drip during extreme cold if the line runs on an exterior wall.
  • Know where the main shutoff is before the next cold snap.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle frozen pipe emergencies at night or on weekends in Athens?

Yes, the phone is picked up around the clock. Tell the dispatcher whether the pipe is still frozen or has already thawed and started leaking, and a same-day visit is arranged when crews are free.

How do I know whether a pipe has split?

Signs include a stain or drip on a ceiling, a damp or swollen baseboard, the sound of running water when everything is off, or a sudden drop in water pressure. If you suspect a split, shut off the main valve.

Is it safe to thaw the pipe myself?

A gentle heat source such as a hair dryer can thaw a pipe with no split, but never use an open flame. If water is already leaking, leave the pipe alone and shut off the water.

How common are frozen pipes in Athens?

Athens has freezing nights every winter, so any exposed pipe can be at risk. The honest local frequency depends on the building, so insulation and heat matter more than any statistic.

Will my insurance cover a frozen pipe?

Often, when it is a sudden and accidental loss, but exclusions apply for neglect or vacancy in some policies. Ask your insurer before filing.
