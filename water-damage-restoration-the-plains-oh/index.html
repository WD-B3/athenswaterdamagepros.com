Water damage in The Plains, Ohio usually starts with a failure inside the house: a split supply line, a water heater that gave out, a washing machine hose or a roof that let rain through. Crews serving ZIP code 45780 pump out standing water, dry the structure and repair what got wet.

The Plains is a census-designated place in Athens County, split between Athens Township and Dover Township, roughly five miles northwest of the city of Athens.

When water is spreading in The Plains

Water spreads faster than most people expect. It runs under baseboards, soaks carpet pad and wicks up drywall within the first hours. The first calls from The Plains are usually about a pipe that burst, an appliance that leaked overnight or a basement that took on water after heavy rain.

Call the 24/7 line and tell the dispatcher where the water came from. Same-day response is offered when crews are available. If you can reach the shutoff valve safely, close it first.

Before we arrive: if water is still spreading

These five steps limit the damage while you wait.

1

Stop the source

Close the shutoff valve at the fixture or the main water valve. The burst pipe page shows how to find it.

2

Power down wet areas

Turn off breakers for wet rooms only if you can reach the panel from dry ground. Otherwise call the electric utility.

3

Lift what you can

Move furniture legs onto foil or blocks, lift rugs and take valuables to a dry room.

4

Keep people and pets out

Stay out of deep water and of any water that came from a drain or a sewer line.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Describe the source, the rooms and how long the water has been there.

Water problems seen in The Plains

Supply line and fixture leaks

A failed braided hose, a loose connection under a sink or a cracked toilet fitting. Most are fixed quickly once the water is shut off, but the wet floor needs drying.

Water heater failures

A tank that corrodes through can release the full contents. See the water heater leak page.

Basement water after rain

Groundwater and downspout runoff can enter through the floor seam. See the basement page.

Storm and roof leaks

Wind-driven rain and damaged shingles let water into attics and ceilings. See the storm damage page.

What is specific to The Plains

The Plains has an Athens postal address and the ZIP code is 45780. The area is served by the Athens City School District, and Athens High School is here. A branch of the Athens County Public Libraries is in the community.

The Wolf Plains Mound Group, listed on the National Register of Historic Places, includes nearly 25 Native American burial mounds and ceremonial circles. Its presence is one reason digging near some parcels draws attention, so ask the county before excavating for drainage work.

The Athens Conservancy has created a nature preserve here, and a planned extension of the Hockhocking Adena Bikeway would cross it. The community sits at an elevation of about 719 feet. For flood risk at a specific address, check the FEMA flood map.

Seasonal risks

Winter freeze-ups hit pipes in unheated garages and exterior walls, so see the frozen pipe page if you notice slow flow in a cold snap. Spring rain tests basements. Summer storms test roofs. A leak that started in one season often gets worse in the next.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in The Plains?

The Plains is served as ZIP code 45780. The same crews also cover New Marshfield in 45766 and Guysville in 45735. If your address is outside those, call and ask.

Is emergency water removal available in The Plains?

Yes. The line is answered 24/7, and same-day response is offered when crews are available, including nights and weekends.

Which county permits apply in The Plains?

The Plains is in Athens County. Rebuild work that needs a permit goes through the county or township office that covers your parcel, so ask them before the rebuild.

Can restoration stop a wet basement from coming back?

No. Restoration dries and repairs. A drainage or foundation contractor addresses the cause.

How do I check whether my property is in a flood zone?

Use the FEMA flood map for your address. Your insurer can also tell you whether flood coverage applies.
