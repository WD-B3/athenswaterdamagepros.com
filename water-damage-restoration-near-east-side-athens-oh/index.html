Water Damage Restoration in the Near East Side, Athens, OH

Brick streets, 1930s homes and a lot of low ground near the Hocking River. This is how water damage gets handled there.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132
45701

The Near East Side is one of the older residential parts of Athens, ZIP code 45701. It sits northeast of Uptown and Ohio University. Water damage here comes from the usual household failures, such as a split supply line or a water heater that gave out, and from groundwater and river-season rain in the lower streets.

If water is in the house now, close the shutoff if you can reach it from dry ground, then call (740) 231-9132 and describe what you see.

What the city says about this neighborhood

The City of Athens describes the Near East Side as roughly 32 blocks northeast of Uptown, bounded on the east by US 33. Earlier city planning documents describe mostly low-density, owner-occupied single-family homes with a median build year of 1930 and brick streets. Apartments cluster along Home Street, Lincoln Street, Hudson Avenue, Meadow Lane and East State Street.

The 2040 plan adds that the neighborhood has a National Register historic residential district, an elementary school, the public library and the Hockhocking Adena Bikeway.

Flood context, stated carefully

The Athens 2040 plan, which the city also labels this area Sunnyside, says "much of the neighborhood is built in the 100-year flood plain." That is the city's planning statement, not a FEMA determination for any one house.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. A mapped floodplain covers part of an area and says nothing certain about a given basement. To check a specific address, look it up on the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask the city floodplain office.

The Hocking River was moved south of the city center in a project that began in 1969, and floods before that were frequent. That history is about the river and the campus. It is not evidence about any particular street here.

Drying in older plaster-and-lath houses follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the public industry standard for water damage restoration. FEMA publishes the flood maps mentioned here. Naming a standard describes a method and is not a claim that any person holds a certification.

Safety in a wet basement

Keep these in mind while you wait for help.

  • Do not enter a basement with standing water near the panel or outlets.
  • Never operate a breaker while standing in water.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on standing water.

Water problems that fit this kind of housing

Older supply and drain lines

Houses from the early twentieth century can still carry original or patched plumbing. Age alone does not mean a leak is coming, but a stain on a ceiling or a soft spot in a floor deserves a moisture reading.

Basement water after heavy rain

Where the water table is high or the ground is low, rain can come in at the floor seam. The basement cleanup page covers pump-out and drying.

Rented apartments and converted houses

A leak upstairs often shows up in the unit below. The ceiling water page explains what crews check.

Sewer or drain backups

Dirty water is a different job. The sewage backup page explains why it needs protective gear and removal of porous materials.

How extraction and drying work in an older house

A crew pumps or vacuums standing water first. Wet carpet pad and swollen baseboards are removed if they cannot be saved, then air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings match a dry reference reading from an unaffected part of the house. Plaster over lath and old hardwood dry more slowly than new drywall, so readings matter more than appearance.

The structural drying page explains the equipment and the readings.

Documenting damage for your insurer

Photos, a list of damaged items and moisture readings can help describe what happened and what work was done. Whether the loss is covered depends on the cause and on your policy. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flooding from rising water, so ask your insurer before you assume either way.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code in the Near East Side?

The Near East Side is in ZIP code 45701. If your address is just outside the city, call and give the street address so the dispatcher can confirm coverage.

Is emergency water removal available in the Near East Side?

The emergency line is answered 24/7. How fast a crew can arrive depends on where crews are at that moment, so describe the damage and ask what to expect.

Is my house in a flood zone?

The city's plan says much of this neighborhood is in the 100-year floodplain, but that does not tell you about one address. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or ask the city.

Does homeowners insurance cover river flooding?

Standard policies often do not. Ask your insurer about your policy and about separate flood coverage.

Can restoration stop my basement from taking on water again?

No. Restoration dries and repairs. A drainage or foundation contractor addresses the cause.
