Should You File an Insurance Claim for a Small Water Leak in Athens, OH?

A small leak can still turn into a large repair. Here is how to decide whether a claim is worth it.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Athens Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

A dishwasher hose drips, a toilet supply line weeps, a bathroom ceiling shows a ring. The repair looks small, and so does the question: is it worth calling your insurer? The answer depends on the size of the loss, your deductible, your policy and your own situation.

This guide gives you a way to decide. It does not replace advice from your agent, who knows your policy and your history.

Start with the deductible

The deductible is the amount you pay before the insurer pays anything. If the repair costs less than the deductible, a claim returns nothing. If it costs a little more, you may receive only a small payment while opening a claim on your record.

Look at your declarations page to find the deductible for water damage. Some policies use a flat dollar amount and others use a percentage of the home's insured value.

Weigh the cost of the repair

Get a written scope before you decide. A crew can take moisture readings, say what has to be dried or replaced and list the work. That tells you whether you are looking at a dry-out and a patch or a larger rebuild.

A leak that looks small on the surface can hide larger damage. A stain on a ceiling may sit under a wet subfloor, and a swollen baseboard may sit over a wet wall cavity. The wall and drywall page explains how far water can travel.

Think about the claim record

Insurers share claim history through industry databases, and a water claim can affect future premiums or the ability to renew. Some insurers also ask about prior claims on a house when you buy it. A claim you did not need to file can cost more over time than the repair.

That is a reason to ask your agent questions, not a reason to skip a large loss. Never hide a loss from an insurer or give wrong information.

Report versus file

Calling your insurer to ask a question is not always the same as filing a claim, but practice varies. Ask the agent how they record the call. Ask what happens to your record if you report and then withdraw. Some policies also require prompt notice of a loss, so do not wait too long to decide.

If in doubt, document everything now. You can decide later with a clear scope in hand.

Ohio rules that help you

Ohio Administrative Code rule 3901-1-54 sets standards for how insurers handle property claims, including time limits for acknowledging and deciding them. You can ask your insurer for its timeline and contact the Ohio Department of Insurance if you have concerns about how a claim is handled.

Households in older homes in Nelsonville and The Plains that are considering a claim for a leak that has gone on a while should know that many policies exclude continuous seepage. Photos that show the date a problem began can matter.

Source type: Ohio Administrative Code and Ohio Department of Insurance

Pay out of pocket when it makes sense

Many small leaks are cheaper to fix yourself: replace the hose, dry the area, repair a small patch of drywall. If you pay out of pocket, still dry the area fully and keep receipts. A leak that is not dried can turn into a larger problem that is excluded as neglect.

Keep photos and plumber invoices for your records, even if you do not file. They help if the same area fails again.

When filing is the better choice

  • The loss is well above the deductible.
  • Water reached several rooms, a ceiling or a subfloor.
  • The water was contaminated.
  • You cannot live in part of the home.
  • The cost is more than you can afford out of pocket.

In those cases, report it quickly, document it and start mitigation. Insurance exists for the large loss, and a large loss is the reason you pay premiums.

Frequently asked questions

Will one water damage claim raise my premium?

It can, depending on the insurer and the claim. Ask your agent how claims affect your renewal.

Can I call my insurer without filing a claim?

You can ask questions, but practice varies on how calls are recorded. Ask the agent first.

What if the repair costs less than my deductible?

A claim would not return anything. Fix the leak, dry the area and keep receipts.

Does insurance cover a leak I have had for months?

Often not. Many policies exclude continuous seepage. Photos that show when it began help.

How do I find out how big the damage is?

Have a crew take moisture readings and write a scope. Hidden damage is common.
