Water Damage Restoration in the South Side, Athens, OH

Hillside homes, Richland Avenue and a creek that rose fast in August 2026. Here is what water damage looks like on this side of town.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132
45701

The South Side of Athens sits on hillsides south of the Hocking River, with Richland Avenue running through the middle of it. Most callers here have one of two problems: water that came in from outside after heavy rain, or a plumbing failure inside the house. The first needs extraction and often removal of soaked materials. The second is usually faster once the source is closed.

What happened on August 11, 2026

Local reporting says the Hocking River rose from about 3.2 feet on August 10 to about 18.8 feet a day later, and Coates Run flooded Richland Avenue for roughly an hour. Emergency crews made multiple water rescues in the city, and Athens County declared a state of emergency the next day.

The reports describe flooding along the Richland Avenue corridor, including the area near Canterbury Drive. They do not say which individual houses took water, so this page does not either.

Local facts for the South Side

The city's Athens 2040 plan notes that the South Side was mostly annexed in the 1950s from the village of Mechanicsburg. It describes mid to late twentieth century single-family housing, several large off-campus developments on the hilltops either side of Richland Avenue, and the Coates Run floodplain along the avenue itself.

Earlier city planning documents give a median build year of 1971, mostly one-story homes on large wooded lots, with no curbs or sidewalks outside the Richland Avenue corridor.

The city mayor told council on August 24, 2026 that South Side flooding involves several streets whose runoff flows toward Richland Avenue, and that catch basins on Lamar Drive were to be examined. A county sewer project is also connecting roughly 1,300 septic users to the city system.

FEMA publishes cleanup guidance for floods, and ANSI/IICRC S500 covers how contaminated water is classified and handled. Both are public references. The Athens City-County Health Department is the local contact for questions about sewage or septic hazards.

Safety around floodwater

Keep these in mind while you wait for help.

  • Do not walk or drive through moving water.
  • Treat creek and runoff water as contaminated.
  • Stay away from downed lines and wet electrical equipment.

Water problems that fit this area

Runoff and creek overflow

Rain that runs downhill can reach basements and ground floors. The flood damage page explains why creek water is treated as contaminated.

Storm leaks

Wind-driven rain that gets past shingles or flashing shows up as stained ceilings. See the storm water page.

Septic or sewer trouble

Where a home is moving from septic to city sewer, or a drain backs up, the water can be dirty. The sewage page covers safe handling.

Rental houses and apartments

Off-campus units share walls and ceilings. A leak in one can reach the next. The ceiling page helps tenants and landlords.

If water is entering the house

Do these before help arrives.

1

Move to higher ground

If water is rising outside, do not drive through flooded roads and do not wade through moving water.

2

Keep clear of electrical hazards

Do not touch the panel or outlets in wet areas. Call the electric utility if the panel is wet.

3

Treat creek water as dirty

Avoid contact with floodwater, and keep children and pets out.

Extraction and drying after outside water

Floodwater and runoff carry soil and bacteria. Crews pump out the water, remove carpet, pad and insulation that cannot be cleaned, clean hard surfaces and then dry the structure with dehumidifiers and air movers. Household fans and a wet vacuum are not enough for contaminated water.

The water extraction page explains the first stage.

Insurance records

Photos and moisture readings can help document what was wet and how it was dried. Coverage depends on the cause of the loss and on your policy, and standard homeowners policies often exclude flood from rising water. Ask your insurer before work begins if you can.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code on the South Side?

The South Side is in ZIP code 45701. For addresses just outside the city, call and give the street address so the dispatcher can confirm coverage.

Is emergency water removal available on the South Side?

The emergency line is answered 24/7. Crew arrival depends on where crews are at that moment and on road conditions, so describe the situation and ask what to expect.

Was my street flooded in August 2026?

Reports describe the Richland Avenue corridor, but they do not list individual houses. If you are unsure, photograph the damage and call.

Will insurance pay for flood damage?

That depends on your policy. Many standard homeowners policies exclude flooding from rising water, so ask your insurer.

Should I wait for the creek to go down before calling?

No. Stay safe first, then call so the dry-out can be planned while the water recedes.
