How to Choose a Water Damage Restoration Company in Athens, OH

When water is in your home, you will make this decision fast. Here are the questions that separate good crews from bad ones.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Athens Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Water damage is stressful and the clock is running. That makes it easy to sign the first paper a crew hands you. A few questions asked up front can save weeks of trouble. This guide lists what to ask, what to check and what warning signs to watch for.

The list works for any company, including this one. A good crew is happy to answer every question.

Start with the basics: who is coming and are they insured?

Ask for the company's legal name, a local phone number and proof of liability insurance. A crew that works in your home can damage it, and insurance covers that. Ask whether the people on site are employees or subcontractors.

Ohio licenses certain trades, such as plumbing, electrical and HVAC, through the Ohio Construction Industry Licensing Board. Ask which license applies to the work being done, and check the license with the state board. Do not accept a vague answer.

Ask about the process, not just the price

  • How will you find all the wet areas? (Moisture meters and thermal cameras)
  • What equipment will you set, and how many days will it run?
  • How will you tell me when the home is dry? (Daily readings and a final log)
  • What will you remove, and why?
  • Will you take photos and share the documentation with my insurer?

A crew that cannot explain its process in plain words is a warning sign. The structural drying page describes what a good dry-out looks like, and the water extraction page covers the first hours.

Get the scope and price in writing before work starts

A written scope lists the rooms, the work and the equipment. It should say how the price is set, whether by line item or by day. Ask whether the price changes if the job grows, and what triggers a change.

Be wary of a crew that will not put a number or a method on paper, and of anyone who pressures you to sign an assignment of benefits or a blank authorization on the spot. Read every form, and take a photo of it.

Ask whether the company follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. That is a question about the method used, and the company should be able to explain what it means for your job.

Who handles permits in Athens County towns

Athens County towns sit in different jurisdictions. The Plains lies in Athens Township and Dover Township, Chauncey in Dover Township and Nelsonville in York Township. Permit offices and requirements for rebuild work can differ between the city, a village and a township.

Ask any contractor who will pull the permit for rebuild work, which office they pull it from and whether an inspection is required. Do not hire anyone who tells you permits are never needed without naming the office they checked.

Source type: Public reference (Wikipedia), township descriptions

Watch for disaster scams

After storms and floods, out-of-town crews often knock on doors. Some are honest, many are not. The Ohio Attorney General's consumer protection office warns residents to be careful with door-to-door offers after a disaster. Ask for local references, check reviews on independent sites and never pay the full amount upfront.

A reasonable deposit is common for rebuild work. For mitigation, payment is usually tied to completed work or billed through your insurer.

Check the references and the track record

Ask for two recent local jobs you can look at or call about. Check how long the company has operated and whether it answers its phone. A company that answers a call at night and gives you a plain answer is one that will probably do the same during the job.

Be cautious about reviews that all appear in the same week or use the same wording. One honest reference is worth more than a page of five-star posts.

Understand how drying is verified

A professional dry-out ends with readings, not a guess. The crew should record moisture levels on walls, floors and framing every day, compare them with dry reference readings and remove equipment only when the structure reaches its goal. Ask to see the log.

If a crew says the house is dry without readings, ask them to show proof. Hidden moisture leads to odor, swollen materials and repair bills later.

Frequently asked questions

What should a water damage company have before starting work?

Proof of liability insurance, a written scope, a plain explanation of the process and a plan for daily moisture readings.

Does Ohio license water damage restoration companies?

Ohio licenses certain trades such as plumbing, electrical and HVAC. Ask which license applies to the work and check it with the state board.

Should I pay upfront for restoration?

Be cautious. Mitigation is often billed on completion or through the insurer. Ask for terms in writing before you pay anything.

How do I spot a scam after a storm?

Be wary of door-to-door offers, pressure to sign right away, large upfront payments and crews that avoid giving local references.

Can I choose my own restoration company if I file a claim?

Generally yes. Read your policy, and ask your insurer if you are unsure.
