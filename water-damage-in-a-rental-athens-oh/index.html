Water Damage in a Rental in Athens, OH: What Tenants Should Do

You do not own the building, but you still have steps to take, rights to know and belongings to protect.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

By the Athens Water Damage Pros team · Published November 10, 2026

Athens is a rental town. About three in four occupied homes here are rented, so a leak in an apartment or a student house is a common problem. Tenants often do not know where the shutoff valve is, who pays for what or how fast they need to act.

This guide covers the first steps, the written notice, what Ohio law generally says about landlords and tenants, and how to protect your own belongings. It is general information, not legal advice.

The first 30 minutes

  1. Find the water source. If it is a fixture, close the shutoff valve under the sink or behind the toilet.
  2. If you cannot stop it, find the main valve for the unit. If you still cannot, call the landlord or property manager right away.
  3. If water is near outlets, a ceiling light or a panel, stay out of that room and call the electric utility.
  4. Take photos and video of the source and the wet areas before anything is moved.
  5. Move your own belongings out of the water to a dry room.

A ceiling that sags or drips is a hazard. Do not stand under it. The ceiling water page explains how to relieve a bulge safely and why you should wait for a crew.

Tell the landlord in writing

Call the landlord or property manager, then follow up by text or email so you have a record. Include the date and time, what you found, what you did and photos. Ask when someone will come. Keep a copy of every message.

Written notice matters. Ohio law gives tenants certain remedies when a landlord fails to fix a problem after receiving notice, and those steps start with notice. Read your lease and the notice requirements in Ohio Revised Code Chapter 5321, or ask a legal aid office.

Renters in Athens

According to the 2020 census figures reported for Athens, about 74 percent of occupied housing units in the city were renter-occupied and 26 percent were owner-occupied. The city had a 2020 population of 23,849, and Ohio University was chartered in 1804.

Many Athens rentals are older houses split into units and apartment buildings with shared pipes, so water from one unit often reaches the one below. Neighbors in Nelsonville, home to Hocking College, face the same stacked-unit problem near the downtown square.

Source type: Public reference (Wikipedia), 2020 census figures

What Ohio landlords must do

Under Ohio Revised Code 5321.04, landlords must keep the premises fit and in good repair, keep plumbing and supplied appliances in good and safe working order, and make repairs needed to keep the unit livable. The exact rules and remedies are in the statute and in your lease.

The statute is general. A landlord can argue that you caused the damage, and a tenant may owe for damage caused by misuse. That is why photos and written notice matter on both sides.

Safety first in a rental

Keep these in mind while you wait for help.

  • Do not touch outlets or switches near water.
  • Keep away from sagging ceilings.
  • Call the electric utility if the panel is wet.

Who calls the restoration crew?

In most rentals the landlord or property manager arranges the dry-out and repairs. Tenants should still push for fast action, because wet materials get worse each day. If the landlord does not respond quickly and water is still spreading, you may be able to arrange emergency mitigation and send the invoice, but check your lease and ask a legal aid office first.

A crew can dry the unit, take moisture readings and document the scope for the landlord's insurer. The water extraction page explains the first steps.

Protect your belongings

  • Photograph furniture, electronics and clothing before moving them.
  • Lift items off wet floors and onto foil, blocks or a dry surface.
  • Do not turn on electronics that got wet. Let a technician check them.
  • Keep receipts for hotels, meals and cleaning supplies if you must leave.
  • Check your renters insurance policy and report the loss.

The landlord's insurance usually covers the building, not your belongings. Renters insurance covers belongings and sometimes extra living costs, but it often excludes flood. If you do not have it, ask the landlord in writing whether they will cover any losses they caused.

When the unit is not livable

If the unit cannot be lived in, ask in writing about a temporary unit or a rent abatement. Ohio law has specific rules on remedies, and they can be complicated. A legal aid office, such as the one serving southeast Ohio, can explain your options. Do not stop paying rent without advice, because a mistake can lead to eviction.

Frequently asked questions

Who pays for water damage in a rental in Athens?

It depends on the cause. A landlord is generally responsible for building repairs when plumbing fails, while a tenant may owe for damage they caused. The lease and the facts decide.

Should I call the landlord or a restoration company first?

Call the landlord first, because they control access and arrange the work, then call a crew if water is still spreading and no one is responding.

Does renters insurance cover a leak from the unit above?

It often covers your belongings if the cause is a covered event. Read your policy or ask your insurer.

Can I withhold rent because of a leak?

Not without advice. Ohio has formal steps for remedies. Talk to a legal aid office before changing how you pay rent.

What if the landlord ignores me?

Keep sending written notices with photos, and contact a legal aid office or your local housing office for options.
