Water Damage Restoration in the Far East Side, Athens, OH

Single-family streets north of East State Street, with 1920s additions and post-war houses. This is how water damage gets handled there.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132
45701

The Far East Side is the part of Athens north of the East State Street corridor and east of the US 33 bypass. The City of Athens describes it as mostly single-family homes in a suburban layout. It is a quieter residential area than the corridor to its south, and water damage here mostly comes from plumbing, appliances and storm leaks rather than from the street.

How the city describes it

The Athens 2040 plan says the neighborhood, also labeled Sells Place, was created in the 1920s as the Grandview Highland Addition and is home to Sells Park. Most homes date from the post-war baby boom. Residents have raised concerns about sidewalks, crossing East State Street and cut-through traffic.

An earlier city document describes the area east of US 33 and north of East State Street as roughly 25 blocks, predominantly low-density residential, with commercial uses along East State Street.

The corridor and the neighborhood are not the same thing

The 2040 plan says portions of the development areas along East State Street are in the 100-year floodplain. On August 11, 2026, local reporting says principal flooding in the city hit Richland Avenue and East State Street. In late August the mayor said the city planned smoke testing along East State Street to find where groundwater enters storm drains and sewer mains.

None of those sources says the residential streets north of the corridor flooded. This page does not claim that.

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The sources do not report how many Far East Side homes have basements or crawl spaces.

Source type: City of Athens, Athens 2040 plan and Athens Independent, Aug. 12 and Aug. 25, 2026

Water problems that fit single-family streets

Crawl space moisture

Houses built in the 1920s through the 1950s can have crawl spaces that collect water after rain or from a plumbing leak. The crawl space page explains pump-out and drying.

Basement seepage

Water can enter at floor cracks or window wells. See basement cleanup.

Kitchen and bath leaks

Dishwashers, sinks and toilets are common sources. The kitchen page covers cabinets and subfloors.

Storm leaks and gutters

Heavy rain exposes failing gutters and flashing. See storm damage.

Extraction and drying

Crews remove standing water, pull wet insulation and flooring that cannot be saved, and dry the structure with air movers and dehumidifiers. In a crawl space, a vapor barrier and good ventilation matter after the dry-out, but that is a separate building decision.

The extraction page and the drying page explain both stages.

Insurance records

Photos, moisture readings and a written scope can help document a loss. Coverage depends on cause and policy, so ask your insurer, and ask about separate flood coverage if you have not already.

Frequently asked questions

Do you cover every ZIP code on the Far East Side?

The Far East Side is in ZIP code 45701. For addresses just outside the city, call and give the street address so the dispatcher can confirm coverage.

Is emergency water removal available on the Far East Side?

The emergency line is answered 24/7. Arrival depends on where crews are at that moment, so describe the damage and ask what to expect.

Did the August 2026 flooding reach my street?

Reports describe East State Street, not the residential streets north of it. If your home has water, photograph it and call.

Is my crawl space a flood risk?

A crawl space can collect water from rain or leaks. A crew can check moisture and drainage. A foundation contractor fixes the cause.

Do I need flood insurance?

Ask your insurer. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flooding from rising water.
