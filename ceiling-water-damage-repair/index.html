Emergency Ceiling Water Damage Repair in Athens, OH

A sagging ceiling can drop without warning. Keep people out from under it and call the 24/7 line.

☎ Call (740) 231-9132

A wet ceiling is an emergency because the water often sits above where you can see it. If the ceiling is dripping, bulging or sagging, move people and furniture away from beneath it and call the 24/7 line. A sagging section holds a lot of water, and pricking it with a screwdriver over a bucket is the safest way to relieve it if you must.

This page covers the finished ceiling and everything above it, no matter where the water came from. The same stain can be a roof leak, an upstairs bath, a supply line or an air handler, and the first job is figuring out which. Roof leaks are covered in their own section below.

When a ceiling leak is an emergency

Treat it as urgent if water is actively dripping, if the drywall is bulging or sagging, if a light fixture is wet or if the stain is growing. A wet fixture is an electrical hazard, so turn off the circuit from a dry spot or call the electric utility.

Same-day response is offered when crews are available, including nights and weekends. If a storm caused the leak and you cannot get to the roof, a dispatcher can tell you what to do while you wait.

Light fixtures and fans in a wet ceiling

Do not turn on a fixture or fan that is wet or sits under a stain. Switch off the breaker from a dry floor. If you see sparks, smell burning or the ceiling is touching a live wire, leave the room and call 911.

Finding where ceiling water comes from

Water rarely stays where it enters. It runs along joists and pipes before dripping through at a low spot or a seam. The stain you see can be several feet away from the actual source.

Crews trace the path with a moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera. A circle with a darker ring often points to a leak that has dried and returned, which is typical of a slow roof leak or a failing seal at a tub or toilet.

One caller pattern is common. A finished basement ceiling gets a stain after an upstairs toilet leaks, and the stain sits directly below the bathroom. Another is a closet ceiling that drips whenever it rains. Both are solved by finding the source before repairing the ceiling.

Roof leaks and the ceiling below them

A roof leak is the most common outside cause. Water gets in at missing or lifted shingles, flashing around a chimney or vent pipe, a valley or a damaged skylight, then travels down through the decking, insulation and drywall. By the time a stain appears, the attic insulation is usually wet too.

Restoration crews dry the ceiling cavity and insulation and repair the drywall, but a roofing contractor needs to fix the roof. Temporary tarping is a roofing job, and a restoration crew can document the damage while the roofer works. Insurers often ask for dated photos of the roof damage and of the ceiling.

The order matters. If the roof is fixed but the wet insulation is left in place, the ceiling stays damp and can sag or stain again. If the ceiling is repaired before the cavity is dry, paint peels and the stain comes back through. For wind and rain damage to the building itself, see the storm damage page.

How crews dry and repair a water-damaged ceiling

Small, brief leaks can sometimes be dried through a small access hole with directed airflow. Ceilings that sag, bulge or have been wet for days are cut open so wet insulation can be removed and the framing dried.

Air movers and dehumidifiers are set, and readings are checked daily. After the cavity is dry, drywall is patched or replaced, taped, primed with a stain-blocking primer and painted. Textured ceilings take extra time to match.

Roof and gutter leaks on wooded hillsides are common calls from the North Side.

Moisture meter readings and a dry reference reading from an unaffected part of the home are standard practice under ANSI/IICRC S500. They are how a crew shows that a ceiling cavity is dry before it is closed up.

Why older Athens houses see ceiling stains

Houses near downtown and along East State Street are often older, and older roofs, flashing and plumbing runs fail at joints and penetrations. Rented houses near campus see another pattern. A tenant upstairs has a tub overflow or a toilet seal fail, and the water reaches the unit below.

If you are a tenant, call your landlord and photograph the stain. A landlord in Ohio must keep plumbing fixtures in good and safe working order and make necessary repairs under Ohio Revised Code 5321.04. If you are a landlord, a quick call can limit the damage to the unit below.

Frequently asked questions

Do you handle ceiling water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Athens?

Yes. Ceiling leaks do not wait for business hours, and neither does the phone. After-hours and weekend calls get the same same-day response when crews are available.

Should I poke a hole in a bulging ceiling?

If the drywall is full of water and about to fall, a small hole over a bucket relieves the weight and limits damage. Stand to the side, wear eye protection and stay clear of any light fixture.

Can I just paint over the stain?

Painting without finding and fixing the source usually fails. The stain returns through the paint. Dry the cavity, fix the source and then use a stain-blocking primer.

Who fixes a roof leak, a roofer or a restoration company?

A roofer fixes the roof. A restoration company dries the ceiling and repairs the interior. Dated photos help your insurer.

How long does a ceiling take to dry?

Small, brief wetting can dry in a few days. Wet insulation and sagging drywall take longer. Readings tell the crew when it is done.
