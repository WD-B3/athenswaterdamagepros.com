Water Damage Restoration Amesville, OH Near Athens

Amesville sits on Federal Creek, and its 1998 flood still shapes how neighbors think about water. Crews respond to creek water and plumbing alike.

Amesville is one of the smallest villages in Athens County, and it sits on Federal Creek in Ames Township. Crews serving ZIP code 45711 handle water from both directions: the creek outside and the pipes inside. The response is the same, fast extraction, careful drying and clear repairs.

The June 1998 Federal Creek flood

On June 28, 1998, Federal Creek flooded after more than 10 inches of rain. The water damaged most of the businesses and houses on the south side of the village. FEMA-funded demolitions followed, and the cleared land became Gifford Park.

That history is a reminder that a creek can rise quickly after intense rain. If water is climbing near your home, move to higher ground first. The flood damage page explains how floodwater is handled.

What is specific to Amesville

Amesville was laid out in 1837 and its post office was established in 1820. It is named for Fisher Ames, who helped gain federal support for the Ohio Company of Associates. The ZIP code is 45711 and the elevation is about 637 feet.

In 1804 settlers bought 51 books for $73.50 using fur pelts, which became the Coonskin Library. The Coonskin Library Museum opened in May 1994. State Street, which carries State Routes 329 and 550, and Main Street both crossed Federal Creek during the 1998 flood.

In 2007 Amesville became the first village in Ohio to use an aerobic wastewater treatment system. The 2020 census counted 171 residents. For flood risk at your address, check the FEMA flood map.

Source type: Public reference (Wikipedia), village descriptions

After a flood: what crews do first

Crews start by checking safety, then pump or extract standing water. Porous materials that touched floodwater, such as carpet, pad and insulation, are usually removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and dried. Readings track the drying until the structure is ready for repairs.

The water extraction page covers the equipment, and the structural drying page covers the dry-out.

Small village, older buildings

Amesville has no large commercial district, so most calls come from houses, a few small shops and public buildings. A leak in a small building can go unnoticed for a long time because nobody is there every day. Check vacant rooms, outbuildings and basements after any heavy rain.

Many buildings in a village this old have older plumbing, crawl spaces and basements that were never waterproofed. Slow leaks go unnoticed for months. A moisture reading and a look under the house find them. See the crawl space page and the basement page.
